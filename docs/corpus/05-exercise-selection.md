# Choosing exercises around the competition lifts

## The competition lifts come first

You get judged on three lifts, and each one is a skill before it is a stimulus. A squat with a bar path that wanders is not a strong squat waiting for more muscle. It is a squat you have not learned yet. Skills need repetition under conditions close to the ones you will be judged in, which is why the competition lifts get trained often and get trained the way you compete.

In a normal week, the squat, bench and deadlift take roughly two-thirds of the hard sets. Everything else divides the remaining third. When time runs short, that ratio goes up, not down.

## What a variation is for

A variation earns its slot for one of two reasons. Either it attacks a weak point the competition lift cannot reach on its own, or it adds volume to a pattern at a cost the lifter recovers from more easily than another heavy competition set.

Weak-point work is specific. A lifter whose squat collapses forward out of the hole gets paused squats, because the pause forces them to hold position at the point where they lose it. A bench that stalls two inches off the chest gets close-grip work and long pauses. A deadlift that breaks the floor fine and dies at the knee gets block pulls above the knee, which lets them load the part that fails without a hundred easy reps of the part that does not.

Volume variations are the opposite. Front squats and Romanian deadlifts give me a pattern with less spinal fatigue per set than another heavy back squat or conventional pull, so the lifter can carry more total work through an accumulation block.

A variation stops earning its slot when the weak point closes, which the competition lift will tell you. If the squat no longer collapses forward under a heavy triple, the paused squats have done their job and something else can have the time.

## Accessories and how they earn their place

The bar I set is simple. I have to be able to say, in one sentence, which of the three lifts an accessory serves and how. If I cannot, it comes out.

More exercises is not more progress. Every accessory takes recovery from the same account the competition lifts draw on. Ten accessories at three sets each is thirty sets of fatigue paying for work that would have been better spent on the pattern being tested.

When time or recovery runs short, the order I drop things is fixed: single-joint accessories first, then volume variations, then weak-point variations, and the competition lifts last. A week with only squat, bench and deadlift in it is still a training week. A week with only accessories is not.

## Selection changes with the block

An accumulation block tolerates variety because the loads are moderate and the goal is total work. That is where paused squats, block pulls, front squats and the rest belong.

As a meet approaches, selection narrows toward what will be tested. Variations drop out first, accessories thin, and the last weeks look almost entirely like squat, bench and deadlift at the loads, the tempo and the equipment you will use on the platform. By the final two weeks, anything that is not the competition lift is a warm-up.
